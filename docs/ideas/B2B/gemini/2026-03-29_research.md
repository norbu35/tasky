# Research Report: B2B Local Service Marketplace Models

This report explores established B2B local service marketplace models to inform Tasky's B2B pivot strategy. The focus is on platforms that have successfully bridged the gap between marketplace dynamics and enterprise operational needs.

## 1. The Short-Term Rental (STR) Model: Turno (formerly TurnoverBnB)
Turno is the industry standard for automating STR turnovers. Their model is highly relevant for Tasky's "Airbnb Host" segment.

**Core Value Proposition:**
*   **Automation:** Syncs directly with PMS (Property Management Systems) and booking calendars (Airbnb, VRBO) to auto-schedule cleaning when a guest books or checks out.
*   **Quality Control:** Digital checklists that require cleaners to upload photos (e.g., "inside the fridge") before payment is released.
*   **Bidding System:** Hosts post a job, and local cleaners bid, creating a competitive, market-driven rate.

**Revenue Model (Hybrid SaaS + Marketplace):**
*   **Marketplace Fees:** If a host hires a cleaner *through the Turno platform*, Turno takes a ~5% fee from the host and a ~5% fee from the cleaner on every job.
*   **SaaS Subscription:** If a host brings their *own* off-platform cleaner just to use the software (scheduling, checklists), Turno charges a SaaS fee (approx. $8-$10/month per property). The first property is free.
*   **Takeaway for Tasky:** The SaaS fee for BYO (Bring Your Own) cleaners is a brilliant growth loop. It forces hosts to onboard their existing offline supply onto the app, digitizing the market.

## 2. The Restaurant/Retail Model: ResQ
ResQ operates in the high-intensity restaurant repair and maintenance (R&M) space (HVAC, plumbing, electrical). This is relevant for Tasky's medium-complexity categories.

**Core Value Proposition:**
*   **Centralized Operations:** Replaces text messages and fragmented spreadsheets with a single app to submit work orders (under 45 seconds with photos).
*   **Preventative Maintenance:** Auto-schedules recurring tasks (hood cleaning, grease traps) to prevent costly emergency breakdowns.
*   **Asset Tracking:** Digital tags (QR codes) on physical equipment to track repair history and warranty status.

**Revenue Model (SaaS-Enabled Marketplace):**
*   **Restaurant Side (SaaS):** Restaurants pay a recurring membership/SaaS fee for the work order software and analytics dashboard.
*   **Vendor Side (Revenue Share):** ResQ takes a commission from the service providers for generating the lead and handling the invoicing/collections.
*   **Takeaway for Tasky:** B2B customers will pay a SaaS fee just to have a "single pane of glass" for their facilities. The value is in the software workflow, not just the vendor discovery.

## 3. The Commercial Cleaning/Office Model
Commercial office cleaning differs fundamentally from residential C2C because it relies on recurring contracts rather than one-off bookings.

**Core Value Proposition:**
*   **Reliability Guarantees:** Automated backups. If a cleaner calls in sick, the platform automatically routes the job to a backup without the office manager noticing.
*   **Compliance:** Vetted background checks and centralized insurance (General Liability/Bonding) provided by the platform.

**Operational Focus (Anti-Leakage):**
*   To prevent clients and cleaners from cutting out the platform, these marketplaces heavily enforce workflow lock-in. This includes mandatory GPS geofenced check-ins and digital compliance reporting that office managers require for their records.
*   **Takeaway for Tasky:** For office B2B, the platform must own the compliance and the backup guarantee. If Tasky can guarantee an office is never left dirty, the platform fee becomes an "insurance" premium the business is happy to pay.

## Strategic Conclusions for Tasky's B2B Pivot
1.  **SaaS is the Trojan Horse:** B2B businesses want operations software more than they want a directory of workers. Tasky should lead with the software (checklists, automated scheduling, iCal sync) and monetize the software layer directly.
2.  **Bring-Your-Own (BYO) Supply:** Tasky should allow B2B businesses to invite their *existing* cleaners onto the app for free (or for a SaaS fee). Once those cleaners are on Tasky, they can be matched with *other* tasks, solving the platform's supply-side cold start problem.
3.  **The Backup Guarantee:** For B2B, a "No-Show" is catastrophic. Tasky must build an algorithmic "Surge Dispatch" system to guarantee fulfillment, differentiating it completely from Facebook groups or Unegui.
